# KPI Framework — Amman Digital Market

Define 5 KPIs for the Amman Digital Market. At least 2 must be time-based and 1 must be cohort-based.

---

## KPI 1
- **Name:**Monthly Revenue Growth Rate
- **Definition:** This tracks the percentage change in total revenue month-over-month to see business momentum.
- **Formula:** ((Current Month Revenue - Previous Month Revenue) / Previous Month Revenue) * 100
- **Data Source (tables/columns):** order_items.quantity, products.unit_price, orders.order_date, orders.status
- **Baseline Value:**Significant growth spike observed in June 2025 (>150%).
- **Interpretation:**This time-based KPI is essential for understanding seasonality. During my analysis, I noticed a huge surge in June, which helps us plan for inventory before these peak months hit.

---

## KPI 2
- **Name:** Average Order Value
- **Definition:**The average revenue generated per unique order, segmented by product category.
- **Formula:**Total Revenue per Category / Total Unique Orders per Category
- **Data Source (tables/columns):**order_items.quantity, products.unit_price, products.category
- **Baseline Value:**Overall cleaned average is 109.94 JOD.
- **Interpretation:** This cohort-based KPI shows which product segments bring in the most value. I found that certain categories like Books drive higher transactions, helping us decide where to focus our marketing.
---

## KPI 3
- **Name:** Monthly Revenue
- **Definition:** Total monthly revenue generated after filtering cancelled orders and quantity outliers.
- **Formula:** SUM(quantity * unit_price) grouped by Month
- **Data Source (tables/columns):**order_items.quantity, products.unit_price, orders.order_date
- **Baseline Value:**Total Cleaned Revenue: 48,701.50 JOD.
- **Interpretation:** This time-based KPI monitors our financial health. By excluding the "noise" like errors and cancellations, I got a more realistic view of the market's performance.
---

## KPI 4
- **Name:** Revenue by City
- **Definition:** Total revenue generated by customers segmented by their registered city.
- **Formula:** SUM(quantity * unit_price) grouped by customers.city
- **Data Source (tables/columns):** order_items.quantity, products.unit_price, customers.city, orders.customer_id
- **Baseline Value:** Amman is the primary revenue driver, followed by Irbid.
- **Interpretation:** This helps us see our geographical strongholds. Even though Amman dominates in volume, it's important to see how much other cities like Irbid contribute to our total reach.

---

## KPI 5
- **Name:**Revenue Distribution Variance
- **Definition:** Measuring the spread of order values across categories to identify price stability.
- **Formula:**Statistical distribution (Median and IQR) shown via Boxplot.
- **Data Source (tables/columns):** order_items.quantity, products.unit_price, products.category
- **Baseline Value:** Statistically validated with an ANOVA F-stat of 46.3877.
- **Interpretation:** Instead of just looking at averages, this tells me if the sales are consistent. High variance in a category might mean unpredictable pricing or irregular bulk purchases.



## Statistical Validation Analysis

Test 1: One-Way ANOVA
Test: One-way ANOVA (Category vs. Order Value).

Hypothesis: H₀: Mean order values are equal across categories / H₁: Mean values are significantly different.

Results: F-statistic: 46.3877, p-value: 0.0000, Eta-squared: 0.2049.

Interpretation: Since the p-value is zero, I rejected the null hypothesis. My analysis proves that the product category significantly affects spending behavior, explaining about 20% of the variance in the data.
Test 2: Welch’s T-Test
Test: Welch’s T-Test (Amman vs. Irbid Spending).

Hypothesis: H₀: No difference in mean spending between cities / H₁: Significant difference exists.

Results: p-value: > 0.05, Cohen’s d: 0.0002.

Interpretation: This was a surprising find. Even though Amman has way more customers and higher total sales, the Cohen’s d is almost zero. This means the individual spending behavior per order is nearly identical in both cities, and the total revenue difference is simply due to the larger volume of orders in Amman.
